# Level 2 — Blind Layout (player's-eye scene description)

_Prepared by the Producer for the Blind Playtester. This document describes ONLY what a
player can see and physically interact with, zone by zone, in the order zones can be
discovered. All puzzle dependency edges, solution values, clue-to-puzzle mappings, and
design-intent labels have been stripped. The playtester must NOT read
`puzzle-graph.json`, `puzzle-graph-summary.md`, or `validation-report.md` during the
blind pass._

## Premise as the player experiences it

You find yourself in a tower clockmaker's attic workshop, high above a town, in late
golden afternoon light. The whole room reads as one connected machine: linkage rods run
along the ceiling from the only exit — a stair door barred by a heavy mechanism — and
vanish into the walls. Every clock in sight is dead. A gray cat naps in a sunbeam. The
game is near-wordless: no text prompts, no hint system.

---

## Zone 1 — Main Attic (starting zone)

### View: Workbench wall
- A long workbench strewn with loose clock parts — springs, small wheels, screws. None
  of the loose parts can be picked up.
- A heavy flat-blade screwdriver standing in a bench rack. It can be taken.
- A chalk slate propped on the bench, drawn with a wordless diagram: a circle with a
  crank handle sketched on it, ringed by twenty-four chalk tally marks drawn in groups
  of five — four uprights struck through by a diagonal fifth stroke, four such groups
  and four single strokes left over; a line from it to a small toothed circle marked
  XII; meshing that, a larger toothed circle chalked only with a "?"; on the same axle
  as the "?", a small toothed circle marked VIII; meshing that, another toothed circle
  chalked "?"; and finally a circle bearing a single notch and, set clearly apart from
  it on the same rim, one lone tally stroke of the same kind as the twenty-four, with a
  little door sketched beside the circle. Legible in close-up.
- A small iron stove, cold. Lying on the hob: a brass numeral tile engraved **II**. It
  can be taken.
- The clockmaker's coat hanging on a peg. Its pockets can be searched. In one pocket: a
  pocket watch with a single hand on its dial, the hand stopped on **III**; the case
  back is engraved with a small house symbol (⌂). In the other pocket: a numeral tile
  engraved **IV**. Both can be taken; the watch can be inspected in close-up from the
  inventory at any time.
- A wheel barometer on the wall: a round brass instrument with a clock-like dial, a
  needle that does not move, and small engraved sun, cloud and rain pictograms visible
  in close-up. It can be inspected; it accepts no input.

### View: Master clock and workroom door
- A tall longcase clock, floor to shoulder height, a small star (★) engraved on its
  case. It is stopped at exactly 6:00 — hour hand on the VI, minute hand on the XII.
  Its numerals, like every dial in the attic, are standard Roman numerals (IV, IX
  style). The face is legible in close-up.
- A workroom door painted with a large clock dial that has no hands. Around the dial
  are twelve tile sockets, each shaped to seat a numeral tile. Eight tiles are already
  seated: **I, III, V, VI, VIII, IX, X, XII**, each in a socket. Four sockets are
  empty — the ones at the 2, 4, 7 and 11 o'clock positions. Tiles from the inventory
  can be seated in the empty sockets.
- Fixed below the door dial, a small wooden tray holding one loose numeral tile
  engraved **VI**. The tray tile can be lifted and tried in the door's sockets, but it
  cannot be carried away; set down or rejected, it drops back into its tray.
- A packing crate full of straw. Half-buried in the straw: a numeral tile engraved
  **VII**. It can be taken.
- Overhead, linkage rods run along the ceiling from the direction of the stair door and
  vanish into the workroom wall.

### View: Stair door and dormer
- The stair door — the attic's only exit — barred by a heavy horizontal bolt-bar seated
  in a vault-style housing. The housing has **no keyhole** anywhere on it. The bar
  connects upward to the ceiling linkage rods: visibly a remote mechanism. Pulling or
  prodding the bar makes it rattle once against its linkage, identically every time.
- A low bench in the dormer sunbeam. On a cushion on the bench, a gray cat, asleep.
  Something flat is visibly tucked beneath the cushion. Petting the cat gets a purr and
  nothing else. Nudging the cat, or offering it any inventory item, gets the identical
  response every time: one slow blink, and the cat resettles heavier on the cushion;
  offered items are returned unused. There is no variation on repeats.
- The dormer window, looking out over golden town roofs.
- On the dormer windowsill: a numeral tile engraved **XI**. It can be taken.
- Carved into the beam beside the dormer: a small house symbol (⌂), ringed by an
  engraved circle of twelve notches, like the hour positions of a clock face. Legible
  in close-up. (At the start the ring is bare. Later in the game a single clock hand
  may be found lying across it, pivoted at its hub and pointing out at one of the
  twelve notches; when it is there it is there in both the room view and the close-up,
  and it is never there in one and absent from the other.)
- The floorboards below and around the dormer are uniform; nothing about any single
  board stands out. (At the start every board is identical. Later in the game a small
  chalk house mark (⌂) may be found drawn on the face of one of them, in the room view
  and in the close-up alike; the mark is not there at the start, and it is gone once
  that board has been lifted.) Boards can be pried at with a suitable tool. (One board
  in this area, if it ever yields, comes up on a shallow cavity holding a large bronze
  gear wheel — sixty-four countable teeth, "64" stamped on its face. The wheel can be
  taken.)

## Zone 2 — Movement Loft (through the workroom door, once it opens)

### View: Automaton gear wall
- A floor-to-ceiling automaton wall: a carved wooden town scene with jointed figures —
  among them a sun on a slotted track and a watchman figure holding a bell — driven by
  an exposed two-stage gear frame in front of it.
- The gear frame, left to right: a fold-out hand crank carrying a fixed brass pinion
  stamped **XII** (twelve countable teeth); an empty square-arbor post ("post A") that
  carries a small second pinion fixed on the same axle, its eight teeth countable and
  **VIII** stamped around the face of that pinion itself; and a second empty
  square-arbor post ("post B") connected to a cam whose linkage runs into the wall
  behind the mural. With both posts bare, the run from crank to cam reads as a single
  line of drive interrupted by two clear gaps — one at each post — and each gap is
  visibly sized to take a wheel: the crank's pinion and post A's pinion are turning
  against nothing at all.
- The frame's main bearing shows a bloom of rust; the crank rocks a few degrees and
  jams. (If the seizure is ever freed, the crank turns.)
- A gear rack bolted to the wall beside the frame, holding six loose brass gears of
  visibly different diameters. Each has countable teeth and a stamped Arabic tooth
  count: **16, 24, 36, 40, 48, 72**. Any rack gear can be mounted on either empty
  post and taken off again; unmounted rack gears return to the rack and never enter
  the inventory. The bronze wheel from the attic cavity, if found, mounts on either
  post the same way and returns to the inventory when unmounted. The two stamped
  pinions (XII, VIII) are fixed and cannot be removed.
- With the crank able to turn and two gears mounted so that everything meshes (post A's
  gear meshing the crank pinion, post B's gear meshing the VIII pinion), cranking runs
  the mural: the figures move, visibly faster or slower depending on which gears are
  mounted. At the end of each full cam revolution, something inside the wall ratchets
  and slips back with a single clack — the same clack every time.
- Chalked on the frame's own timber cheek, beside the fold-out crank, in the same hand
  as the slate in the attic: a block of twenty-four tally marks grouped in fives,
  standing at the crank end of the frame, and one lone matching tally stroke down at
  the cam end. Readable in the room view and in the close-up.
- Below that block, separated from it by a chalked baseline, a second, empty stretch of
  cheek. While the crank is turning, chalk strokes appear there one after another — one
  full stroke for each complete turn of the crank in the cam's current cycle, grouped in
  fives as they accrue, with any leftover part-turn shown as a single half-height
  stroke. The accrual can be skipped ahead, and the finished block stays drawn and
  readable at a glance after the crank stops. It clears the moment any gear is mounted
  or taken off either post, and starts again from nothing on the next crank. Nothing
  about it ever glows, flashes or changes colour.
- The chimney breast passes through this room. One brick bears a small carved gear
  symbol (⚙) ringed by an engraved twelve-notch circle, legible in close-up. (At the
  start that ring is bare. Later in the game a single clock hand may be found lying
  across it, pivoted at its hub and pointing out at one of the twelve notches; as with
  the ring in the attic, it is either present in both the room view and the close-up or
  absent from both.) The surrounding bricks are uniform; nothing about any single brick
  stands out, at any point in the game. Bricks can be pried at with a suitable tool.
  (One brick here, if it ever yields, comes loose on a recess holding a long-spout oil
  can. The can be taken.)

### View: World-clock row
- A row of four dead wall clocks, every one with its hands removed — bare faces with
  standard Roman numerals and empty center posts. Beneath each clock is an engraved
  brass plate showing a landmark silhouette and a stamped marking, left to right:
  1. a needle-thin spire tapering to a point (recognizable as the Burj Khalifa),
     stamped **+IV**;
  2. an ornate clock-tower silhouette (recognizable as Big Ben), stamped with a small
     **★** and nothing else;
  3. a snow-capped mountain with long symmetrical slopes (recognizable as Mount Fuji),
     stamped **+IX**;
  4. a robed statue holding up a torch (recognizable as the Statue of Liberty),
     stamped **−V**.
  Silhouettes and stamps are legible in close-up. The clocks themselves accept no
  interaction.
- A small parts cabinet. Its top drawer opens; inside is a tin wind-up mouse with a
  butterfly key in its side. It can be taken. Wound and set on the floor, the mouse
  skitters in a small circle, winds down, and returns to the inventory.
- A glass-front display case holding assorted spare clock hands of many sizes. The case
  frame is screwed shut and painted over — clearly visible in close-up. It can be
  inspected; it does not open and accepts no input.

## Zone 3 — Behind the Great Dial (reachable only after a wall panel in the Movement Loft opens — contents below are what becomes visible then)

### Single view
- The tower's great dial, seen **from behind**: a huge translucent disc glowing amber
  with transmitted afternoon daylight. The two hands are visible as dark silhouettes
  through the glass. Every numeral on the face reads backwards — mirror-writing, each
  glyph crisply reversed — legible in close-up.
- A hand-setting crank on the motion works below the dial. Turning it moves the hands;
  the minute hand clicks through five-minute steps. The crank always turns.
- A winding drum with a square key socket in its face and a drive weight hanging low on
  its line. The drum's bearing is rusted dry and squeals at any movement. A key fitted
  to the socket seats but will not turn while the bearing squeals.
- A long pendulum, hanging dead still. It can be pushed; once pushed, it takes up a
  swing and keeps swinging.
- The strike train and chime hammers, connected to the ceiling linkage rods — the same
  run of rods that leaves this chamber toward the attic and the stair door. The rod
  line can be followed end to end.
- A floor hatch in the platform, locked shut, faced with four brass combination
  wheels. Each wheel is engraved **I through XII** and spins freely with a click at
  each numeral, holding its position. Above the wheels, left to right, four small
  pictogram header plates: an ornate clock-tower silhouette with a small ★ beside it;
  a needle-thin spire; a robed statue holding a torch; a snow-capped mountain. Setting
  the wheels produces no reaction from the hatch; the wheels keep whatever positions
  they are left in.

## Zone 4 — Strongroom under the platform (reachable only after the floor hatch releases — contents below are what becomes visible then)

### Single view
- A small strongroom beneath the dial platform, reached by steps down through the
  hatch.
- A large winding key with a square bit, hanging on a hook. It can be taken.
- A brass shop tag hanging on a nail, the kind a shopkeeper hangs on a door. On it: a
  miniature clock dial with hands **permanently fixed in place** — the hour hand a
  third of the way past the VII, the minute hand pointing at the IV — and beneath the
  dial, a small pictogram of a door. It can be taken and inspected in close-up from
  the inventory at any time.
- A second cat cushion and an empty saucer on the floor.
- A shelf of old ledgers and a cloth-wrapped, unfinished pocket watch. They can be
  inspected; nothing can be taken from the shelf.

## Global interaction notes (player-observable behavior)

- Items picked up go to a visible inventory and can be tried on any hotspot. Failed
  item uses simply return the item; nothing is ever destroyed or lost by a wrong
  attempt.
- Tiles seated in the door dial: a tile seated in a wrong socket (or a wrong tile in
  any socket) whirs, stalls, and pops back out — inventory tiles return to the
  inventory, the tray tile drops back to its tray. Correctly seated tiles stay seated.
  Unlimited attempts.
- Prying at floorboards or bricks: a board or brick that does not yield simply does not
  budge, identically every time. No damage is done; attempts are unlimited.
- All wheels, dials and hands keep their positions between attempts. There are no
  timers anywhere, and nothing that opens ever re-locks.
- The cat's cushion can be lifted only when the cat is not on it. Beneath it: a second
  pocket watch — a single hand on its dial, stopped on **IX**, a small gear symbol (⚙)
  engraved on the case back. It can be taken and inspected from the inventory at any
  time. (If the cat ever comes off its cushion, it thereafter naps beside the stair
  door instead, and answers to petting and offers exactly as before.)
- The great movement behind the dial is evidently restorable. Under some as-yet-unknown
  condition, the strike train fires: chimes sound, the ceiling rods articulate along
  their whole run, and the bar at the stair door lifts — and stays lifted. If the cat
  is by the door when this happens, it stands and stretches. The stair door then opens
  onto the tower stairs.
