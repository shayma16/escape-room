# Level 1 — Blind Layout (player's-eye scene description)

_Prepared by the Producer for the Blind Playtester. This document describes ONLY what a
player can see and physically interact with, zone by zone, in the order zones can be
discovered. All puzzle dependency edges, solution values, clue-to-puzzle mappings, and
design-intent labels have been stripped. The playtester must NOT read
`puzzle-graph.json`, `puzzle-graph-summary.md`, or `validation-report.md` during the
blind pass._

## Premise as the player experiences it

You wake in a wizard's cabin deep in dark woods. It is night; a clear sky with a bright
moon and stars is visible through the windows. The front door's bolt is wrapped in
living thorn-vines, and a shallow basin shaped like a crow's beak is set into the door
at lock height. A live crow watches you from a cage on a tall stand. The game is
near-wordless: no text prompts, no hint system.

---

## Zone A — Main Cabin Room (starting zone)

### View: Hearth wall
- A fireplace with a cold pile of ash. On very close inspection, something glints
  faintly inside the ash.
- An iron poker leaning against the hearth. It can be picked up.
- A mantel clock with a full ring of Roman numerals I–XII. Its hands can be moved
  freely. (If the hands are set to XII, a small wooden carved crow pops out on a spring,
  then retreats. Nothing else observable happens.)
- A small hand bellows hanging by the hearth. Scratched into its handle: a rune
  (upward triangle with a bar) next to the Roman numeral I.
- Scorched into the hearth lintel: a different rune (plain upward triangle) next to the
  Roman numeral II.
- A worn armchair.
- A floor rug, which can be slid aside freely. Beneath it: a trapdoor locked by a
  three-dial rotary lock. Each dial carries eight embossed moon-phase shapes (crisp
  raised silhouettes — new moon through crescents, quarters, gibbous phases, full).
  Dials rotate freely and hold their position.

### View: Study wall
- A writing desk with a large grimoire lying open. The grimoire cannot be taken but can
  be browsed. Its visible spreads:
  - **Page A:** four element-style runes drawn beside small pictograms — plain upward
    triangle beside a flame; plain downward triangle beside a wave; upward triangle
    with a bar beside a cloud; downward triangle with a bar beside a mountain.
  - **Page B:** dense arcane writing (illegible). In the margin: a sun symbol drawn
    beside a small ring pictogram, and a crescent symbol drawn beside a small coin
    pictogram.
  - **A page bookmarked with a black feather:** a wordless pictographic sequence — a
    moonflower blossom over a mortar; a crescent-stamped piece of metal being filed
    into slivers; a black feather drifting INTO an open upturned hand; a flame
    pictogram beside the numeral III; a spiral with an arrowhead pointing
    counterclockwise, with five dots spaced along the spiral.
  - **Two other spreads:** a zodiac wheel; an anatomical diagram of a bird. Both richly
    illustrated.
- On the wall, a triptych of three small framed paintings, each of the same tree at
  night, differing in detail:
  - one painting has ONE crow in the tree and a waxing crescent moon;
  - one has TWO crows and a full moon;
  - one has THREE crows and a waning gibbous moon.
- A dead flowerpot. On its clay side: a rune (downward triangle with a bar) beside the
  Roman numeral III.
- A bookshelf of old books. They can be flipped through; nothing is inside them.
- A heavy inner door in this wall, barred by a press-lock: four large rune tiles
  (matching the four rune glyphs seen around the cabin), each of which can be pressed.
  Wrong sequences produce a dull knock and the tiles reset.

### View: Entry wall
- The front door, sealed as described (thorn-vines over the bolt; crow's-beak basin at
  lock height). The bolt cannot be moved while the vines hold it.
- A rusted, bent key hanging on a hook beside the door. It can be taken. In close-up,
  its bit is visibly snapped off and plain.
- A window showing moonlit woods. Etched into the windowsill: a rune (plain downward
  triangle) beside the Roman numeral IV.
- The crow's cage on its tall stand. The cage door has a tiny STAR-SHAPED keyhole,
  clearly visible in close-up. A brass feed cup is clipped inside the bars. If the
  player reaches into the cage (for a feather or anything else), the crow snaps once,
  turns its back, and guards its wing — the identical closed-off pose on every repeat,
  with no variation.

## Zone B — Potion Workshop (behind the rune-barred inner door, once it opens)

### View: Bench
- A cauldron of clear water over a fire pit. A large floor bellows sits beside the pit;
  pumping it visibly raises the flame, which cycles through three heights and back to
  low. Three runes — I, II, III — are embossed on the cauldron rim, each with an ember
  channel that lights up when the flame is at that stage.
- A long-handled ladle rests in the cauldron. It can be stirred clockwise or
  counterclockwise, any number of turns, and released.
- A workbench with a heavy mortar and pestle, fixed in place.
- Dried herbs hanging overhead (scenery).

### View: Cabinet wall
- A locked ingredient cabinet with two inset recesses on its face: one circular recess
  ringed with rays, one crescent-shaped recess. Objects can be tried in the recesses;
  ill-fitting or wrong items pop back out and return to the player.
- A shelf of assorted stoppered potion bottles — at least five clearly different bottle
  shapes, each with a small pictogram label (a sleeping face, a snowflake, a sprouting
  seed, and others). All stoppers are fused shut with old wax and cannot be opened.
- A brass astrolabe / star-globe on a pedestal: a rotating pointer that can be aimed at
  any of six engraved star-pattern plates (a cross of stars; three aligned stars with
  four flanking stars; a seven-star ladle; a hooked chain of stars; a five-star arc; a
  zigzag row). A small drawer in the base is shut.
- A window framing the night sky between trees: three bright stars in a short straight
  diagonal row stand out, with fainter stars around them.

## Zone C — Hidden Cellar (down the ladder, once the trapdoor opens)

### Single view
- An earthen root cellar, dim.
- A barrel, its lid nailed shut. A slight gap at the rim looks pryable.
- A root shelf with a small drawer. Inside the drawer: a tarnished spoon, its bowl
  stamped with a small crescent hallmark. It can be taken.
- A heavy sliding shelf flush against the far wall. Above it: a pulley with a taut rope
  running to the shelf's runners, ending at an empty hanging hook.
- A vertical light shaft in the ceiling, closed by a wooden shutter. A winch mechanism
  beside it has an empty square socket where a handle should fit.
- A tilting mirror on a floor stand near the shaft. It rotates through three click-stop
  positions. On the floor at the third position: faint old scratch marks.
- If the shutter is ever opened, a shaft of moonlight drops vertically to the cellar
  floor near the mirror stand. With the beam on, rotating the mirror visibly sweeps the
  reflected beam across the cellar; at the third click-stop the beam is thrown through
  a low doorway-shaped gap (only present once the sliding shelf has moved — see Zone D).

---

## Zone D — Walled Alcove (behind the sliding shelf, once it rolls aside)

### Single view
- A stone niche.
- A planter of moonflowers, buds tightly closed. (If moonlight is present anywhere in
  the cellar, the buds visibly stir and tremble. If a beam of moonlight is thrown into
  the alcove itself, the buds open into pale blossoms; an open blossom can be picked.)
- A carved wooden crow statue holding a small key in its beak — the key's bit is
  star-shaped. It can be taken.
- A dusty empty shelf ledge.

---

## Global interaction notes (player-observable behavior)

- Items picked up go to a visible inventory and can be tried on any hotspot or combined
  with each other. Failed item uses simply return the item; nothing is ever destroyed
  or lost by a wrong attempt.
- The cauldron accepts inventory items dropped into it. Stirring and releasing the ladle
  resolves whatever is in the pot: on a bad mixture the water puffs gray, any added
  items float up intact and return to inventory, and the pot reverts to clear water. On
  some as-yet-unknown correct procedure, the liquid instead takes on a pearlescent
  sheen with a slow spiral pattern on its surface.
- All locks and dials allow unlimited attempts and keep their positions; there are no
  timers anywhere.
- If the crow is ever freed from its cage, it flies up to the rafters, preens, and a
  single black feather drifts down within reach; the crow stays perched in the cabin
  thereafter (and later tends to perch on the door lintel, above the basin).
