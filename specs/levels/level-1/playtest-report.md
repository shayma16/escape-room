# Level 1 "The Wizard's Cabin" — Blind Playtest Report

_Blind Playtester pass, 2026-07-04. Blind pass conducted against `blind-layout.md` only;
`puzzle-graph.json` was opened AFTER the blind findings below were drafted, solely for the
intended-vs-discovered annotations in the final section. Official difficulty 6.0/10;
designer blind-solve estimate 45–75 min._

---

## 1. Verdict at a glance

The level is fair, well-signposted, and structurally sound from a first-contact
perspective. Nothing in it reads as *unfair*; every stall I hit was eventually resolved by
information that was already in the room. The two mid-game branches interleave well and
never left me without a live lead. The main frictions are **misdirection overhead** — the
brass feed cup and the cuckoo-crow clock both actively build wrong theories rather than
merely wasting a glance — and a blind solve time I estimate at **65–105 minutes (median
~80)**, i.e. at or slightly above the top of the designer's 45–75 window. For a
"challenging level 1" that is acceptable, but the estimate in the ledger should be nudged.

---

## 2. Blind playthrough narrative (what a first-timer actually does)

### Opening (Zone A, first ~15 min)
- The crow's-beak basin + live crow pairing lands instantly: "something crow-related goes
  in the door." Good premise clarity for a wordless game.
- **Rusted key:** taken immediately, tried on the cage within the first minute. The
  close-up (snapped, plain bit vs. clearly star-shaped keyhole) killed the theory in
  seconds. Fair-and-cheap red herring. I retried it later on the winch's square socket
  (it's bent and tool-shaped) — 2–3 min total lifetime cost. Fine.
- **Reaching into the cage:** the snap + wing-guard pose is where my first significant
  wrong theory formed. My honest first read was NOT "the feather must be freely given" —
  it was "the crow is protecting something under its wing; I need to distract or FEED it,"
  because a brass feed cup is sitting right there inviting exactly that. I logged "find
  bird food" as a standing goal and later tried the dried herbs (scenery, uninteractive),
  a moonflower blossom, and silver shavings on the cup. See §4, friction F1.
- **Clock:** setting XII pops a wooden crow — which convinced me the clock was part of the
  crow puzzle chain. I revisited it three times across the session (times matching the
  triptych crow counts, times matching moon phases, once more after freeing the crow).
  ~6–8 min sunk. See friction F2.

### First locks (Zone A → B and A → C)
- **p01 rune door (~8–12 min blind):** the four rune+numeral marks are a clean scavenger
  loop; the flowerpot and windowsill marks needed genuine close-up hunting (good — this is
  the intended "learn to inspect everything" tutorial beat). Grimoire page A was
  confirmation, not requirement; I pressed by numeral order without needing the element
  meanings. Satisfying first "big" solve.
- **p02 moon trapdoor (~6–10 min blind):** rug→trapdoor discovery is immediate; the
  triptych link (3 paintings / 3 dials, moons on both) took under two minutes. ALL of the
  friction is in silhouette precision: waxing vs. waning crescent are mirror images and I
  entered one wrong combination from misjudging which side of the painted crescent was
  lit. Fair, but the art requirement (unmistakable orientation at iPhone close-up size —
  already flagged as Validator A5) is genuinely load-bearing; if the art is soft here this
  puzzle's feel degrades from "precise" to "guessy."

### Workshop branch (Zone B)
- **p03 astrolabe (~3–6 min):** the three-in-a-row bright stars in the window snapped to
  the Orion plate quickly. I did detour to the grimoire's **zodiac wheel** first ("which
  constellation does the wizard care about?") — ~3–4 min over-reading a decoy. Fair-tricky,
  and per the graph this was a known watch-item (A4). It cost time but never felt like the
  game lied to me.
- **Potion bottle shelf (~5–7 min sunk):** five labeled bottles scream "shortcut — pour
  one in the door basin." I tried prying wax with the poker and (later) softening a
  stopper over the cauldron flame. Nothing budged and the identical fused-wax read on all
  five eventually said "scenery." Fair red herring, but it is the single most
  puzzle-shaped non-puzzle in the level; expect most players to spend 5–10 min here.
- **p05 ash sift (2–4 min if the glint is noticed early):** I poked the ash on general
  principle ("poker exists, ash exists"). A player who misses the glint could stall the
  cabinet later; the sun slot demanding a round object is what would send them back
  hunting (+5 min in that path).
- **p04 cabinet (~4–8 min):** with ring and coin in hand this is quick, and the
  shape-fit redundancy (round band → rayed circular recess; crescent-stamped coin →
  crescent recess) means zero alchemy knowledge was needed — I placed by shape alone.
  My one wobble: before the astrolabe gave me the coin, the crescent-hallmarked SPOON was
  my only crescent object and I tried it in the crescent slot (it popped back out —
  penalty-free, so this cost <1 min). Once the coin existed the ambiguity self-resolved.

### Cellar branch (Zone C)
- **p06 barrel pry (1–2 min):** nailed lid + visible pry gap + poker in hand. Instant.
- **p07 counterweight (2–4 min):** the taut rope running from pulley to shelf runners with
  an empty hook is excellent wordless machine language. Heavy iron weight from the barrel
  → hook. No friction.
- **p08 winch (2–3 min once the crank exists):** the empty square socket parked itself in
  my memory the moment I saw it; when the astrolabe drawer produced a crank the match was
  instant. Classic satisfying cross-zone key-in-lock.
- **p09 mirror (~2–4 min):** floor scratch marks at detent 3 pre-teach the answer even
  before the beam exists — nice. One experiential wrinkle: I set the mirror to detent 3
  BEFORE the shelf had moved, and with the beam on, the beam strikes where the shelf/wall
  is with no result. For ~a minute this read as "broken puzzle" before I reframed it as
  "the beam wants to go THROUGH there" — which then actually reinforced moving the shelf.
  Emergent signposting, but only if the beam-vs-shelf visual makes the blockage legible
  (see recommendation R4).
- **p10 bloom (1–2 min):** the buds trembling under ambient beam light is the best
  wordless progress cue in the level. When the redirected beam opened the blossoms I knew
  immediately what ingredient one was. Flow moment.

### Convergence and endgame
- **p11 free the crow (3–5 min for the mechanics themselves):** star key from the alcove
  statue → star keyhole was frictionless, and the feather drifting down retroactively
  reframed the wing-guarding animation ("it wouldn't be taken; it had to be given").
  Elegant in hindsight — but see F1: prospectively, the cage interaction had already sent
  me on a food hunt.
- **p12/p13 (file + spoon; grind blossom, ~3–6 min):** the recipe page's
  "crescent-stamped metal filed into slivers" pictogram plus the spoon's matching crescent
  hallmark made the file+spoon combination clean once the file existed. Grinding the
  blossom in the mortar was immediate.
- **p14 brew (~8–15 min):** **the pictogram page IS sufficient to infer the procedure**,
  which was a specific question for this pass. My decode path: three ingredient
  pictograms → drop paste, shavings, feather in pot; flame+III → pump bellows until rim
  rune III's ember channel lights (the rim runes lighting per stage is exactly the
  feedback needed); spiral+arrowhead+five dots → five counterclockwise stirs. I failed
  twice (once resolving at flame stage II, once stirring clockwise — my top-down read of
  the spiral's direction wavered) and succeeded on attempt three. Because failure returns
  everything intact, this played as *experimentation*, not punishment. Two decode risks
  worth noting: the five dots could initially read as "five ingredients" (with only three
  ingredients available this dies fast), and CCW-on-the-page vs. CCW-in-the-scene must be
  visually unambiguous (see R3).
- **p15–p17 (~3–6 min):** the phial from the cabinet answered my standing "how do I carry
  this" question. Pour into beak basin → vines wither → bolt → out. The freed crow
  perching on the door lintel above the basin is a lovely silent nudge — by endgame I had
  already connected basin↔crow, but for a player who hadn't, that perch does real work.

## 3. Pacing assessment

- **Zone A opener:** strong. Two independent exits (rune door, trapdoor) are both fully
  solvable from starting-room information, so the first 20 minutes always have a live
  thread. No dead zone.
- **Mid-game branch interleaving:** this is the level's best structural feature and it
  lands. Cellar-first players hit the winch (needs workshop crank) and go back upstairs
  *with a concrete want*; workshop-first players hit the crescent slot (coin already in
  hand from the astrolabe) and the recipe's cellar-sourced ingredients. Both cross-links
  (crank down, spoon up) create purposeful traffic rather than aimless wandering. I never
  experienced a moment with zero leads.
- **Clue pile-up:** mild and benign. The recipe page front-loads five pictograms that
  don't pay off for 30+ minutes; I re-read it four times. That's fine — it functions as
  the level's North Star, and the black-feather bookmark correctly flags it as The
  Important Page.
- **Potential dead zone (only one found):** a player who neither pokes the ash early nor
  solves the astrolabe promptly can idle in Zone B with a locked cabinet and fused
  bottles. Both escapes from this lull (ash glint; window stars) are close-up-inspection
  rewards, consistent with the level's core skill, so I rate this a soft lull, not a hole.
- **Difficulty curve within the level:** ramps correctly from a player's-eye view —
  scavenger code → shape-precision code → cross-zone machine chains → multi-parameter
  procedure. The hardest cognitive load (p14) lands last, where the player is most
  invested. As a series opener billed "challenging at level 1," 6.0 feels honest;
  experienced escape-room players will run ~60 min, genre newcomers will exceed 90.

## 6. Intended-vs-discovered annotations (written AFTER opening puzzle-graph.json)

- **Discovered as intended:** triptych→dials mapping; poker→ash and poker→barrel; Orion
  plate; weight→hook; crank→winch; scratch-marks→detent-3; star key→cage; full brew
  procedure from the recipe page alone; basin as final pour target. The dependency spine
  was communicated by scene language alone — no edge required designer knowledge.
- **Blind uncertainty resolved correctly by the design:** my two open questions during the
  pass ("where does a crescent coin come from?" and "what carries the potion?") are both
  answered by item yields I could not see in the layout (astrolabe drawer → coin + crank;
  cabinet → file + phial). Both arrive on mandatory paths, so the questions answer
  themselves in normal play. Good.
- **Spoon-in-crescent-slot:** I tried it; the graph's intended answer is the coin. The
  pop-back-out rejection plus the recipe's "filed into slivers" pictogram resolved it
  cheaply. Intended dual-crescent ambiguity works as a fair micro-puzzle.
- **Alchemy sun=gold/moon=silver (flagged borderline in the register):** confirmed
  unnecessary in practice — I solved p04 purely by shape fit and never invoked the
  alchemical fact. The redundant path carries the puzzle by itself.
- **Crow refusal (D3):** the *terminal* tone succeeded at its stated goal — I never ground
  the cage hoping for escalation. What the design underweights is that the **feed cup**
  keeps the wrong theory alive independently of the refusal animation (F1). D1 treats the
  cup as a draught mis-pour case only; in blind play its bigger cost is earlier and
  theory-level.
- **Clock (rh-clock):** the graph calls it "not pure noise" because the numeral ring
  backs p01. From the player side that backup is nearly weightless, while the XII
  crow-pop misdirection is heavy. Intended cost: a glance. Actual cost: three visits.
- **I never used:** grimoire page A (element meanings) for p01 — numerals suffice; the
  bird-anatomy spread (correctly cheap decoy); the clock as a numeral reference.
- **Validator A4 watch-item (zodiac/astrolabe theme collision):** confirmed real,
  confirmed time-cost-only, ~3–4 min. No action needed beyond keeping the wheel visually
  non-systematic (no highlighted constellation).

## 7. Recommended design changes (severity-ranked; friction descriptions, not redesigns)

1. **R1 — Medium. Defuse the feed-cup theory magnet (F1).** The feed cup needs a
   player-visible signal that feeding is not a path — e.g. the crow's existing terminal
   refusal also playing when items are offered at the cup, per D1's block-behavior option
   (a). Whatever the Designer chooses, offered-item attempts at the cup should get the
   same categorical "never" tone as the reach-in, not a neutral item-return that reads as
   "wrong food, keep trying." This is the single largest avoidable time sink.
2. **R2 — Medium. Cap the clock's misdirection (F2).** The XII crow-pop invites repeat
   investment. Any small de-escalation preserves the charm while releasing the player —
   the Designer might consider whether the pop should telegraph "toy, not mechanism" on
   repeat (e.g. increasingly dusty/creaky, same outcome). Severity is medium only because
   the cost recurs across the whole session.
3. **R3 — Medium (art/dev requirement, not a design change).** p14's counterclockwise cue
   must be orientation-consistent between the page pictogram and the in-scene cauldron
   view; the stir-direction affordance on the ladle should make CW vs. CCW unmistakable
   from the camera's actual angle. My one "unfair-adjacent" flicker in the whole run was
   wondering whether the page's CCW meant *my* CCW.
4. **R4 — Low (art requirement).** When the beam hits the unmoved sliding shelf at
   detent-3 (F5), render the blockage legibly (bright spot ON the shelf face) so the beat
   reads as "blocked, look here" rather than "bugged." This turns a wobble into a hint.
5. **R5 — Low.** Reconfirm Validator A5 at asset time: waxing/waning silhouette
   orientation on BOTH the triptych and the dials is the difference between p02 feeling
   precise and feeling arbitrary on an iPhone screen.
6. **R6 — Informational.** Update the ledger's blind-solve estimate to ~60–100 min for a
   first-time player. No difficulty change recommended; 6.0 stands.
